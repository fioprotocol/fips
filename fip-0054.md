# Abstract
This FIP establishes Stellar as the home chain of FIO Protocol ("FIO 2.0") and defines the migration of the FIO registry (FIO Domains, FIO Handles and their public-address mappings) from the FIO chain to a Soroban smart contract, `FioRegistry`.
* Stellar becomes the canonical chain for FIO Handle and FIO Domain ownership and for public-address resolution.
* The registry is migrated by snapshot: every live FIO Domain and FIO Handle, with its owner, expiration and mapped public addresses, is imported into `FioRegistry` with its original expiration.
* Owners claim their migrated names on Stellar by proving control of the FIO public key that owns them on the FIO chain.
* The FIO chain and Stellar coexist during a migration period; after cut-over the FIO chain registry becomes read-only.
* The FIO Protocol funds the registry migration (contract development, audit, snapshot and import). Payment integrations built on top of the Stellar registry (wallets, exchanges, payment flows, FIO Requests on Stellar) are funded separately, including through Stellar ecosystem grants.

Proposed `FioRegistry` contract functions (Soroban):
|Function|FIO chain equivalent|Description|
|---|---|---|
|register_domain|regdomain|Register a FIO Domain for one year|
|set_domain_public|setdomainpub|Make a FIO Domain public or private|

# Terminology
* **FIO 2.0** - FIO Protocol with Stellar as its home chain.
* **FIO chain** - the current FIO Protocol blockchain (Antelope-based), where the registry lives in the `fio.address` contract.
* **FioRegistry** - the Soroban contract that holds the FIO registry on Stellar.
* **Migration authority** - the Stellar account set as `admin` of `FioRegistry`, controlled by the FIO Protocol through a multisig of FIO Foundation and Block Producer representatives. It imports the snapshot and upgrades the contract.
* **Snapshot** - an export of the `fionames` and `domains` tables of `fio.address` at a fixed FIO chain block.
* **Claim** - the act of an owner moving a migrated FIO Handle or FIO Domain to a Stellar account by signing with the FIO private key that owns it.

# Motivation
FIO Protocol makes crypto payments usable by replacing public addresses with human-readable FIO Handles (`name@domain`). Over 2.5 million FIO Handles have been registered since mainnet launch, and over 245,000 were registered in the last 12 months. The value of a FIO Handle is realised when it is used to pay, and payments happen on payment networks, not on the FIO chain.
* Stellar is a network built for payments: fast finality, low fees, native assets and stablecoins, anchors and on/off-ramps, and broad wallet and exchange support.
* Soroban provides a smart-contract platform that can host the FIO registry with the same rules as the FIO chain.
* Running the registry where payments settle removes a separate chain, token and wallet integration from every payment flow. Wallets and payment apps on Stellar resolve FIO Handles with a single contract call.
* A single home chain lets the FIO Protocol concentrate infrastructure, security and integration effort.

# Specification
## Home chain
Stellar is the home chain of FIO 2.0. `FioRegistry` on Stellar mainnet is the canonical registry of FIO Domains and FIO Handles once cut-over (below) completes.

## FioRegistry contract
The reference implementation is [fioprotocol/fio.registry.soroban](https://github.com/fioprotocol/fio.registry.soroban). It is built and released with [stellar-expert/soroban-build-workflow](https://github.com/stellar-expert/soroban-build-workflow), so each release carries a build-provenance attestation and is shown as source-verified on StellarExpert.

### Data model
Mirrors `fio.address`:
|FIO chain table|FioRegistry record|Fields|
|---|---|---|
|domains|Domain|name, owner (Stellar address), is_public, expiration|
|fionames|Handle|name, domain, owner (Stellar address), expiration, addresses[{chain_code, token_code, public_address}]|

### Validation
Identical to `fio.common/fio_common_validator.hpp`:
* FIO Handle: 3-64 characters total; name and domain use `[a-z0-9-]` with no leading or trailing hyphen; input is lowercased.
* FIO Domain: 1-62 characters, same character rules.
* Chain code: 1-10 characters of `[A-Za-z0-9$]`. Token code: same, or `*` for all tokens on a chain. Codes are stored uppercase.
* Public address: 1-128 characters, no spaces. Up to 200 mapped public addresses per FIO Handle.

### Processing
* Registration and renewal terms are one year.
* `register_handle` requires the domain to be live and either public or owned by the registrant.
* `resolve` returns the public address for the exact chain code and token code, and falls back to the `*` token code of the chain.
* Expired FIO Handles and FIO Handles on expired FIO Domains do not resolve. An expired name can be registered again.
* `transfer_handle` clears mapped public addresses, as `xferaddress` does on the FIO chain.
* All state-changing functions require authorization by the owner (`require_auth`), except `migrate_*`, `set_admin` and `upgrade`, which require the migration authority.
* Events are emitted for registration, address changes, transfers and renewals.

### Exception handling
|Error condition|Trigger|Contract error|
|---|---|---|
|Invalid FIO Handle|Format fails validation|InvalidHandle (1)|
|Invalid FIO Domain|Format fails validation|InvalidDomain (2)|
|Invalid chain code|Format fails validation|InvalidChainCode (3)|
|Invalid token code|Format fails validation|InvalidTokenCode (4)|
|Invalid public address|Format fails validation|InvalidPublicAddress (5)|
|Invalid expiration|Migrated expiration is not in the future|InvalidExpiration (6)|
|FIO Domain not found|Domain is not registered|DomainNotFound (7)|
|FIO Domain expired|Domain expiration has passed|DomainExpired (8)|
|FIO Domain already registered|Domain is registered and live|DomainAlreadyRegistered (9)|
|FIO Domain not public|Registrant does not own a private domain|DomainNotPublic (10)|
|FIO Handle not found|Handle is not registered|HandleNotFound (11)|
|FIO Handle expired|Handle expiration has passed|HandleExpired (12)|
|FIO Handle already registered|Handle is registered and live|HandleAlreadyRegistered (13)|
|Public address not found|No mapping for chain code and token code|AddressNotFound (14)|
|Too many public addresses|Handle already has 200 mappings|TooManyAddresses (15)|

## Migration
### Snapshot
* The Block Producers agree on a snapshot block on the FIO chain.
* All rows of `domains` and `fionames` with `expiration` later than the snapshot block time are exported, including mapped public addresses.
* The snapshot file and its SHA-256 hash are published. Anyone can reproduce it from a FIO chain node at the snapshot block.

### Import
* The migration authority imports every snapshot row with `migrate_domain` and `migrate_handle`, preserving the original expiration, followed by the mapped public addresses.
* Until claimed, migrated names are owned by a claim contract that holds them on behalf of the FIO public key that owns them on the FIO chain.

### Claim
* The owner signs a claim message containing the FIO Handle or FIO Domain and the destination Stellar account with the FIO private key (secp256k1) that owns it in the snapshot.
* The claim contract verifies the signature against the FIO public key recorded in the snapshot (Soroban `secp256k1_recover`) and transfers the name to the Stellar account.
* Wallets integrated with FIO perform the claim on behalf of their users.

### Coexistence and cut-over
* During the migration period the FIO chain remains the source of truth. Changes on the FIO chain after the snapshot block are replayed to `FioRegistry` by the migration authority.
* At cut-over, new registrations, renewals and address mappings move to `FioRegistry`. The FIO chain `fio.address` contract is set to read-only.
* Dates for the snapshot, the migration period and cut-over are set by the Block Producers in a follow-up FIP before the snapshot is taken.

## Funding
* The FIO Protocol funds the registry migration: `FioRegistry` and claim contract development, security audit, snapshot, import, and the infrastructure that runs the migration.
* Payment integrations on top of the Stellar registry are funded separately, including through Stellar ecosystem grants. These include wallet and exchange resolution of FIO Handles, payment flows that use FIO Handles on Stellar, and FIO Requests on Stellar.

# Rationale
* **Stellar** is chosen because FIO Handles exist to make payments easier, and Stellar is a network built for payments with the wallets, anchors and stablecoins that FIO users pay with.
* **Snapshot and import** preserves every live name with its original expiration, so no owner loses a name or paid time because of the migration.
* **Claim by FIO key signature** lets owners move to Stellar without trusting a third party with their names, and without the migration authority guessing which Stellar account belongs to which FIO owner.
* **Same validation rules** keep every existing FIO Handle valid on Stellar and keep integrators' parsing logic unchanged.
* **A migration authority with upgrade rights** is required to import the snapshot and fix defects found during the migration period. The authority is a multisig, and it can be replaced by a governance contract once the migration completes.
* **Fees** are out of scope for the reference contract. Registration and renewal fees on Stellar, and their denomination, are specified in a follow-up FIP together with the FIO token on Stellar.

# Backwards Compatibility
* Existing FIO Handles and FIO Domains remain valid and keep their expirations.
* During the migration period the FIO chain API (`/get_pub_address`, `/get_fio_names` and others) continues to work unchanged.
* After cut-over, integrations resolve FIO Handles through `FioRegistry` on Stellar. FIO SDKs add a Stellar resolver so integrators switch by upgrading the SDK.
* FIO Requests, FIO Data and bundled transactions remain on the FIO chain until a follow-up FIP specifies them on Stellar.
